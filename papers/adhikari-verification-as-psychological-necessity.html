Verification as Psychological Necessity: Reframing GenAI Accountability Through Basic Psychological Needs

Prasil Adhikari — Johannes Kepler University and Pro2Future GmbH, Linz/Graz, Austria

Download PDF All accepted papers
Abstract

Position paper

We usually treat verification of generative-AI output as quality control: a check that catches errors before they spread. That is true, but it is not the whole story. Drawing on Self-Determination Theory, we argue that verification is also how practitioners hold on to the three basic psychological needs that keep work meaningful: competence, autonomy, and relatedness. When we skip verification, or reduce it to a quick rubber stamp, we lose more than accuracy. We lose the sense that we are still good at the work, that the work is still ours, and that the people who rely on us can still trust it. We offer a simple way to decide when to hand a task to GenAI and when to do it ourselves, we ask how organizations should measure competence in this new era, and we argue that making verification effortless does not help us learn, whereas learning to act as an orchestrator, with real system-design skill, does.

Keywords: Generative AI, accountability, de-skilling, professional autonomy, self-determination theory, verification, mind perception, HCI practice
